Most business owners know roughly what they spend to win a customer.
The advertising, the discounting, the time spent on enquiries that go
nowhere.

Far fewer know what the second sale is worth. That is the number that
decides what you can afford to spend on the first one.

The arithmetic is not complicated. Every sale after the first carries
no acquisition cost, so the margin on it is almost entirely yours. If
one customer in five buys again, each new customer is worth about 20%
more than the first sale suggests. If one in two buys again, you can
afford half as much again for exactly the same customer as a
competitor whose customers never come back -- and still be the more
profitable of the two.

That is the whole game. Not cheaper advertising. A customer worth more.

Advertising gets more expensive every year, and no campaign trick
reverses that. What you can change is what happens after the enquiry
arrives, and whether there is anything worth buying next.
